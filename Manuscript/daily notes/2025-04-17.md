- [ ] I think the scope of the amblyopia development paper could be expanded a bit to consider potential mechanisms that produce an ocular dominance shift in strabismus. Strabismus is not sufficient to produce amblyopia, and what our model seems to have recapitulated is strabismus with “alternating fixation.” This reflects segregation of eye responsivity among V1 neurons such that neurons become primarily responsive to one eye or the other rather than responsive to stimuli from either eye. Amblyopia tends to occur with there is a strong fixation preference and the non-fixating eye is suppressed. Potential mechanisms could include top-down modulation or interocular suppression (which presumably involves reciprocal inhibition between each eye’s pathway). Could we use this model of strabismus to test these possible contributors to weigh in on which best explains development of amblyopia? I think this aspect would certainly boost the impact and novelty of this paper.
